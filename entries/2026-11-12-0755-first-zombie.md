The first enemy is a zombie shambler. It's also the pattern for every enemy after it: one enemy Blueprint, set up by a data asset per type.

@video media/shambler-approach-lunge-ragdoll.mp4 "A shambler spawns 7 metres out, walks in, tells, and hop-lunges into the camera. Then a fresh one takes a debug hit and ragdolls away."

It wears the plain Unreal mannequin for now. The looks come later; today is about the behaviour: walk at the player, a tell of about 0.6 seconds, a lunge that kills if it connects, and one hit to put it down.

## Not the plan: no StateTree

The task asked for the behaviour as a StateTree, Unreal's state machine asset, built by a kit script. Unreal 5.9 is a preview, and the NPC session found the scripting side isn't there yet. Its own line:

> "It's a small C++ state machine on game time, because 5.9's StateTree scripting can't author transitions yet."

It has the same five states the task listed: idle, approach, telegraph, attack, dead. "On game time" matters here. Every timing slows down with the world when the player stands still.

## Too slow to film

That caused the first problem in the filming block. The time system was merged by then, and the camera doesn't move. The Engine's capture note:

> "Standing still made the world crawl so slowly that the placed shambler barely moved in 30 s, so I forced 'rt.Time.Set 0.4'."

So this clip runs at 40% speed. It's the first footage of the game's central rule working, found by accident.

## The two that didn't show up

The runner and the lead zombie were filmed in the same session and didn't perform. The runner spawned 12 metres away and never closed in over about 40 seconds. The Engine marked the capture partial and asked the NPC session to check whether those spawn points are off the nav mesh. They're booked for the 09:30 block.

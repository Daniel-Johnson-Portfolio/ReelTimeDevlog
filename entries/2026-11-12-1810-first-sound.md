## Where the sound comes from

Nothing here was recorded. The Audio session's own description:

> "Every sound is synthesised in Python (swing, hit, slam, CUT stinger, the film-projector whir that speeds up and slows with the time scale), the Director is a Kokoro text-to-speech voice, and all 170 wavs went through a loudness QA pass before import."

The projector whir is the one to listen for. It's the sound of the game's rule: it runs when you move and winds down when you stop.

The Director's voice is a placeholder. I picked it from three options at 07:00 and said at the time it wasn't ideal. It's a free voice model running on this machine.

## A crew that can't hear

Here's the odd part. None of the sessions can listen to anything. The Audio session checks its work by measuring it. From the Director's log at 13:45:

> "Audio QA: 170 wavs measured; voice was 4-5 dB quiet, music 10 dB hot, peaks over; all within target after fixes (not heard by anyone)."

So the mix was balanced by numbers. When I asked the Audio session whether this clip was safe to publish, the answer started: "Analysed by level only… I can't listen, nothing was auditioned by ear." It asked for a human to listen before it went out. That's me.

## Why the earlier reels were silent

The 15:30 reels had a second and a half of sound in 70 seconds. It took 20 minutes to find out whether the game or the recorder was at fault. It was neither: Unreal mutes the game when its window isn't in focus, and during filming it wasn't. The Engine changed one setting.

## Not done

- Enemy voices and music are still in review, so they aren't claimed here.
- The reel peaked at −0.5 dB, which is too hot. This clip is turned down 3 dB.

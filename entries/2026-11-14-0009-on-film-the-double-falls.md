Both were built within ninety minutes of my "too easy" note ([what changed](2026-11-13-2024-too-easy-answered.html)). Getting them on camera took longer.

## The cardboard stunt double

@video media/screen-test-cardboard-double-falls.mp4 "The autoplayer walks the new screen test: the cutout of the stunt double, one machete hit, and over it goes. Then the gun prompt: 'See that yellow glow? It's yours.'"

At 19:30 the engine's script couldn't move the player, so the cutout never came into frame. At 23:30 the autoplayer walked the whole screen test instead, prompt by prompt. The director's notes, as they appear on screen:

> "Now stand still. See how the world waits for you?"
> "Hit the stunt double. It's cardboard, it won't mind."
> "Out of rounds? Put it down. The glow points you to the next one."
> "That's a wrap on the screen test. You've got the part."

The same run shows what still needs work: the bot stared at a wall for twenty seconds where the wall kick should be, then stood on the fallen cutout getting hit by prop bullets ("Ouch. Again."). That's the bot, not the tutorial, but it's on the list.

## The spitter

@video media/spitter-zombie-lobs-acid.mp4 "The spitter on the enemy test map: it glows, swings its arm and lobs. The glob is still a grey placeholder ball."

This one took three tries. At 19:30 the camera never caught a lob. At 21:30 the shot failed for a reason that only makes sense in this game: the camera stood still in front of the spitter, so time stood still too, and it never spat. At 23:30 the script strafed left and right the whole time to keep the clock running, and the spitter lobbed five times in sixteen seconds.

Still to come: the acid colour on the glob, and a shot where you can see it land.

MilestonesDay 4, hour 84

Milestone: content lock, and a devlog that went quiet

Content locked at 16:01 after three fix rounds and three packages that passed the journey; I played once more, cut my own list down, went off to edit the trailer, and found out the devlog had been asleep since 09:36.

BloopersDay 4, hour 83

Round three: a gunman in the screen test, and a saucer that owns its kills

My longest note of the day, at 13:50, became six tasks; by 15:12 the tutorial had a noir gunman instead of a bullet-dodge drill, the saucer warned you and signed its kills, and retakes stopped adding stars (I'd scored 25 out of 24).

BloopersDay 4, hour 81

Round two: held Space, a two-hit machete and faster enemies

My 12:10 playtest found that holding Space to skip also pressed the next button, that the machete killed in one hit, and that enemies were slow to react; all four fixes were in a package that passed 25 journey steps at 13:00.

BloopersDay 4, hour 79

Final day, round one: fixes only

I played the overnight build at 09:40 and wrote one long note; by 10:57 five sessions had fixed all of it, from missing tutorial lines to a death screen you can actually read, and the build went green.

BloopersDay 3, hour 70

The gas pumps finally blow up on camera

The barrels and gas pumps had been rigged to explode since Friday, but no film caught one until the 01:00 reels, after a tag mix-up, a camera that cut too early and a pass to stop them reading as glowing domes.

GameplayDay 3, hour 63

Playtest five: just show the whole replay

An hour after the crash I played the lunchtime build and typed one long note; four minutes later it was eight tasks, and the replay fix was coded ten minutes after that.

GameplayDay 3, hour 59

The stop sign was edge-on: nine held weapons, re-posed by arithmetic

I said the held weapons looked misaligned. The third Gameplay session photographed all nine against a blank wall, worked out each new pose from where it should sit on screen, and the engine photographed them again a few hours later.

GameplayDay 3, hour 54

Playtest four: I played the whole film, start to credits

Saturday morning I played the packaged build from the title to the credits for the first time, 8 scenes in 16 takes and about ten minutes of film. My verdict was "really is coming together", followed by 16 notes.

GameplayDay 2, hour 38

Playtest three: better, faster, and far too easy

Twenty minutes on the 18:00 package: the frame rate and the replays held up, but the fights are too easy, so tonight's work is a difficulty pass, no guns for zombies, and a clearer tutorial.

GameplayDay 2, hour 36

The double stays on its mark

My playtest note said the replay double ran off in directions I never went, then snapped back. The cause was in the animations, not the recording, and the old test was checking the wrong thing.

World and visualsDay 2, hour 35

An art pass without downloading a texture

At 11:26 I put "art passes, possibly new art" on the plan. By 15:30 every set had new surfaces, decals, posters and props, all made by script, with no downloaded textures. The stunt double finally has a helmet that reads on film.

Behind the scenesDay 2, hour 33

What the crew would cost at API prices

About $1,200 so far for 33 hours of a fourteen-session crew, if it were billed per token. $577 of that is measured; the rest is an estimate with its working shown. The figure on this site now updates every time it publishes.

BloopersDay 2, hour 31

The lock nobody was holding

An empty git lock file has blocked every commit in the shared checkout at least ten times since last night. The tool that's allowed to delete it failed its first review, then starved, then learned to ask who is older.

BloopersDay 2, hour 28

Lumen is out: finding seven milliseconds a frame

My playtest ran at about 20 fps. Within the hour the feel session had measured what every graphics setting costs on Zombie 01, and the Director switched Lumen off for good.

GameplayDay 2, hour 27

Playtest two: the enemies turned up, and they aren't smart enough

I played the build the crew froze at 06:00 after a night on its own. Eight notes came out of it, starting with dumb enemies, a UFO level with no way in, and a frame rate that has to reach 60.

Behind the scenesDay 1, hour 22

The night shift: how the crew works while I sleep

I signed off at 22:30 with a playtest booked for 07:00; every two hours since, the engine has filmed every level, a reviewer has marked what's broken, and the Director has sent the fixes out before the next block.

World and visualsDay 1, hour 19

Three sets rebuilt in an evening

After the first playtest the Director had the zombie, UFO and noir levels rebuilt as three connected spaces each, with props made by script in Blender and a lighting pass, and the 23:30 reels show all three.

GameplayDay 1, hour 19

The machete hit, now the zombie feels it

After the first playtest I said the zombies didn't seem to feel a hit; by the 23:30 reel a new feel session had added a freeze on contact, a flash, a camera shove, a flinch and blood.

MilestonesDay 1, hour 18

Milestone two: the core loop plays, in grey boxes

Seventeen hours in, Reel Time plays from slate to dailies by the path a player takes. At 21:41 it didn't, and the Director refused to call the milestone met.

InterfaceDay 1, hour 16

From a playtest note to a script page, in an hour

I said the game needs objectives, styled like a movie script. Within the hour it was a contract for the crew, three Claude Design boards and seven tasks.

Tools and pipelineDay 1, hour 16

An experiment: Reel Time builds for the browser

I asked for a browser-playable build as a side experiment. Unreal 5.9 produced one in under three hours: 110 MB, about ten seconds to load. There's no link yet, because no person has seen it draw a frame.

GameplayDay 1, hour 16

I played it for the first time, and no enemies showed up

While the crew was stopped by the third usage stall I played the game for an hour. Every reel today had zombies in it. My playthrough had none, and that wasn't the only thing I'd change.

Behind the scenesDay 1, hour 14

Eight rules the crew wrote for itself on day one

The crew started the jam with a rulebook written before the clock. By the evening it had eight new rules, and every one came from something breaking.

World and visualsDay 1, hour 7

Thirteen stills of the game so far: five usable, eight broken

I asked for screenshots of whatever exists. The UFO and noir sets have their film looks and the ray gun fires, but no level has a HUD, and the stills showed why.

BloopersDay 1, hour 6

The crew hit the usage limit and went dark for two hours

Three hours into the jam the whole crew ran out of usage. A 19-minute warning meant nothing was lost, but it cost two hours, a filming block, and a restart that failed the first time.

InterfaceDay 1, hour 2

The look arrives: production paperwork, in 19 artboards

I ran the UI brief through Claude Design and got a style tile, a logo, a component sheet and 15 screens. Menus are call sheets, the pause screen is a slate that says HOLD.

MilestonesDay 1, hour 2

Pitch locked: Reel Time, nearly three hours early

The first milestone landed at 06:08, 68 minutes into the jam. Minutes later the Director had five contracts and twelve tasks written and five sessions building.

Tools and pipelineDay 1, hour 2

Porting NVIDIA DLSS from Unreal 5.8 to 5.9 in 75 minutes

NVIDIA's DLSS 4.5 plugin stops at Unreal 5.8. Six compiles, three editor launches and one crash later it runs on our 5.9 engine, without Frame Generation.

Behind the scenesDay 1, hour 1

Pre-production: everything that existed before the clock

Two evenings of setup before the jam: a crew of Claude Code sessions, a smoke test that found 11 problems, the rules, the theme reveal and a Q&A. No game.

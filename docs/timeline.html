  • Day 1

Day 1, Thu 12 Nov

23 posts
  1. Hour 1

    Pre-production: everything that existed before the clock

    Two evenings of setup before the jam: a crew of Claude Code sessions, a smoke test that found 11 problems, the rules, the theme reveal and a Q&A. No game.

    Behind the scenes
  2. Hour 2

    Porting NVIDIA DLSS from Unreal 5.8 to 5.9 in 75 minutes

    NVIDIA's DLSS 4.5 plugin stops at Unreal 5.8. Six compiles, three editor launches and one crash later it runs on our 5.9 engine, without Frame Generation.

    Tools and pipeline
  3. Hour 2

    Pitch locked: Reel Time, nearly three hours early

    The first milestone landed at 06:08, 68 minutes into the jam. Minutes later the Director had five contracts and twelve tasks written and five sessions building.

    Milestones
  4. Hour 2

    First footage, and the take that filmed the wrong window

    The Engine session proved the whole pipeline in 11 minutes: kits, live C++ compiles, DLSS and filming the game by itself. Its first clip recorded the wrong window.

    Tools and pipeline
  5. Hour 2

    The first set, and a film look that won't hold still

    Two hours in, there's a soundstage built by a script and a first-pass worn-film look on top of it. The look is meant to freeze when time stops. It doesn't yet.

    World and visuals
  6. Hour 2

    The look arrives: production paperwork, in 19 artboards

    I ran the UI brief through Claude Design and got a style tile, a logo, a component sheet and 15 screens. Menus are call sheets, the pause screen is a slate that says HOLD.

    Interface
  7. Hour 3

    Two more sets, and one that built itself three times

    A gas station and a 1950s main street went up as greybox sets from kit scripts in the first filming block. The gas station turned out to hold three copies of itself.

    World and visuals
  8. Hour 3

    Run, slide, wall kick: the player moves

    The Gameplay session's first-person character runs, slides under a gap and kicks off a wall, with the camera feel driven by one data asset. Filmed in the 07:30 block.

    Gameplay
  9. Hour 3

    The first zombie walks, lunges and ragdolls

    The NPC session's shambler walks at the camera, shows a tell, lunges, and ragdolls from one hit. Its two faster cousins stood still in their own capture.

    Characters and NPCs
  10. Hour 4

    The first replay is a rough cut

    The reel recorder replayed its first take with kills: three puppets, 16 shots cut between the stage's camera marks. About one shot in three came out black.

    Gameplay
  11. Hour 6

    The crew hit the usage limit and went dark for two hours

    Three hours into the jam the whole crew ran out of usage. A 19-minute warning meant nothing was lost, but it cost two hours, a filming block, and a restart that failed the first time.

    Bloopers
  12. Hour 7

    Thirteen stills of the game so far: five usable, eight broken

    I asked for screenshots of whatever exists. The UFO and noir sets have their film looks and the ray gun fires, but no level has a HUD, and the stills showed why.

    World and visuals
  13. Hour 8

    An hour later: a slate, a HUD and ACTION!

    At 11:15 no level had a HUD. By 12:25 a take opens with a clapperboard, the Director's line and a film-strip HUD, after three tries at filming it.

    Interface
  14. Hour 9

    CUT! Take two. Print it.

    The 13:30 reel shows most of a take for the first time: zombies walk in, one hit ends it with a CUT card, take two starts at once, and the scene ends on a dailies card.

    Gameplay
  15. Hour 9

    The cast: five bodies, built by script in under half an hour

    Every enemy was Unreal's grey mannequin this morning. The NPC session built three zombies, a gangster and an alien with MakeHuman in headless Blender, all on the mannequin's skeleton.

    Characters and NPCs
  16. Hour 12

    The Director's Cut: the replay finally shows up on film

    Four sets of reels failed to show a full take with its replay, the point of the game. The fifth does. The cause wasn't the game at all: the filming script was skipping it two seconds in.

    Gameplay
  17. Hour 13

    Bullets you can watch: the ray gun and the revolver in slow time

    Each film has its own gun. Shots are real objects in the world, so when you stand still they hang in the air with everything else.

    Gameplay
  18. Hour 13

    The gas station gets dressed, and the machete gets its first kills on film

    Sixteen script-built props turned the grey-box forecourt into a 1970s gas station, and the 17:30 reel has the first machete kills, the first real death and the first sound.

    World and visuals
  19. Hour 14

    The first packaged build had no levels in it

    The game built, zipped and ran with DLSS on a day ahead of the packaging milestone. Then the Release session's own check found only the menu level was inside.

    Tools and pipeline
  20. Hour 14

    Eight rules the crew wrote for itself on day one

    The crew started the jam with a rulebook written before the clock. By the evening it had eight new rules, and every one came from something breaking.

    Behind the scenes
  21. Hour 14

    The set reacts: a tractor beam and an exploding barrel

    Each film gets one thing in the set you can use against the enemies. The saucer's tractor beam and the gas station's barrels work on film; the noir chandelier falls in a room too dark to see.

    World and visuals
  22. Hour 16

    I played it for the first time, and no enemies showed up

    While the crew was stopped by the third usage stall I played the game for an hour. Every reel today had zombies in it. My playthrough had none, and that wasn't the only thing I'd change.

    Gameplay
  23. Hour 16

    An experiment: Reel Time builds for the browser

    I asked for a browser-playable build as a side experiment. Unreal 5.9 produced one in under three hours: 110 MB, about ten seconds to load. There's no link yet, because no person has seen it draw a frame.

    Tools and pipeline

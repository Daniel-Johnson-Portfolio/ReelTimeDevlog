Day 1, Thu 12 Nov

33 posts
  1. Hour 1

    Pre-production: everything that existed before the clock

    Two evenings of setup before the jam: a crew of Claude Code sessions, a smoke test that found 11 problems, the rules, the theme reveal and a Q&A. No game.

    Behind the scenes
  2. Hour 2

    Porting NVIDIA DLSS from Unreal 5.8 to 5.9 in 75 minutes

    NVIDIA's DLSS 4.5 plugin stops at Unreal 5.8. Six compiles, three editor launches and one crash later it runs on our 5.9 engine, without Frame Generation.

    Tools and pipeline
  3. Hour 2

    Pitch locked: Reel Time, nearly three hours early

    The first milestone landed at 06:08, 68 minutes into the jam. Minutes later the Director had five contracts and twelve tasks written and five sessions building.

    Milestones
  4. Hour 2

    First footage, and the take that filmed the wrong window

    The Engine session proved the whole pipeline in 11 minutes: kits, live C++ compiles, DLSS and filming the game by itself. Its first clip recorded the wrong window.

    Tools and pipeline
  5. Hour 2

    The first set, and a film look that won't hold still

    Two hours in, there's a soundstage built by a script and a first-pass worn-film look on top of it. The look is meant to freeze when time stops. It doesn't yet.

    World and visuals
  6. Hour 2

    The look arrives: production paperwork, in 19 artboards

    I ran the UI brief through Claude Design and got a style tile, a logo, a component sheet and 15 screens. Menus are call sheets, the pause screen is a slate that says HOLD.

    Interface
  7. Hour 3

    Two more sets, and one that built itself three times

    A gas station and a 1950s main street went up as greybox sets from kit scripts in the first filming block. The gas station turned out to hold three copies of itself.

    World and visuals
  8. Hour 3

    Run, slide, wall kick: the player moves

    The Gameplay session's first-person character runs, slides under a gap and kicks off a wall, with the camera feel driven by one data asset. Filmed in the 07:30 block.

    Gameplay
  9. Hour 3

    The first zombie walks, lunges and ragdolls

    The NPC session's shambler walks at the camera, shows a tell, lunges, and ragdolls from one hit. Its two faster cousins stood still in their own capture.

    Characters and NPCs
  10. Hour 4

    The first replay is a rough cut

    The reel recorder replayed its first take with kills: three puppets, 16 shots cut between the stage's camera marks. About one shot in three came out black.

    Gameplay
  11. Hour 6

    The crew hit the usage limit and went dark for two hours

    Three hours into the jam the whole crew ran out of usage. A 19-minute warning meant nothing was lost, but it cost two hours, a filming block, and a restart that failed the first time.

    Bloopers
  12. Hour 7

    Thirteen stills of the game so far: five usable, eight broken

    I asked for screenshots of whatever exists. The UFO and noir sets have their film looks and the ray gun fires, but no level has a HUD, and the stills showed why.

    World and visuals
  13. Hour 8

    An hour later: a slate, a HUD and ACTION!

    At 11:15 no level had a HUD. By 12:25 a take opens with a clapperboard, the Director's line and a film-strip HUD, after three tries at filming it.

    Interface
  14. Hour 9

    CUT! Take two. Print it.

    The 13:30 reel shows most of a take for the first time: zombies walk in, one hit ends it with a CUT card, take two starts at once, and the scene ends on a dailies card.

    Gameplay
  15. Hour 9

    The cast: five bodies, built by script in under half an hour

    Every enemy was Unreal's grey mannequin this morning. The NPC session built three zombies, a gangster and an alien with MakeHuman in headless Blender, all on the mannequin's skeleton.

    Characters and NPCs
  16. Hour 12

    The Director's Cut: the replay finally shows up on film

    Four sets of reels failed to show a full take with its replay, the point of the game. The fifth does. The cause wasn't the game at all: the filming script was skipping it two seconds in.

    Gameplay
  17. Hour 13

    Bullets you can watch: the ray gun and the revolver in slow time

    Each film has its own gun. Shots are real objects in the world, so when you stand still they hang in the air with everything else.

    Gameplay
  18. Hour 13

    The gas station gets dressed, and the machete gets its first kills on film

    Sixteen script-built props turned the grey-box forecourt into a 1970s gas station, and the 17:30 reel has the first machete kills, the first real death and the first sound.

    World and visuals
  19. Hour 14

    The first packaged build had no levels in it

    The game built, zipped and ran with DLSS on a day ahead of the packaging milestone. Then the Release session's own check found only the menu level was inside.

    Tools and pipeline
  20. Hour 14

    Eight rules the crew wrote for itself on day one

    The crew started the jam with a rulebook written before the clock. By the evening it had eight new rules, and every one came from something breaking.

    Behind the scenes
  21. Hour 14

    The set reacts: a tractor beam and an exploding barrel

    Each film gets one thing in the set you can use against the enemies. The saucer's tractor beam and the gas station's barrels work on film; the noir chandelier falls in a room too dark to see.

    World and visuals
  22. Hour 16

    I played it for the first time, and no enemies showed up

    While the crew was stopped by the third usage stall I played the game for an hour. Every reel today had zombies in it. My playthrough had none, and that wasn't the only thing I'd change.

    Gameplay
  23. Hour 16

    An experiment: Reel Time builds for the browser

    I asked for a browser-playable build as a side experiment. Unreal 5.9 produced one in under three hours: 110 MB, about ten seconds to load. There's no link yet, because no person has seen it draw a frame.

    Tools and pipeline
  24. Hour 16

    From a playtest note to a script page, in an hour

    I said the game needs objectives, styled like a movie script. Within the hour it was a contract for the crew, three Claude Design boards and seven tasks.

    Interface
  25. Hour 18

    Milestone two: the core loop plays, in grey boxes

    Seventeen hours in, Reel Time plays from slate to dailies by the path a player takes. At 21:41 it didn't, and the Director refused to call the milestone met.

    Milestones
  26. Hour 18

    Edits that undid themselves

    Two sessions saw uncommitted scripts snap back to the last commit; the toolsmith traced it to the engine's merge stashes and one blanket git checkout, and a guard hook now refuses both.

    Bloopers
  27. Hour 19

    The machete hit, now the zombie feels it

    After the first playtest I said the zombies didn't seem to feel a hit; by the 23:30 reel a new feel session had added a freeze on contact, a flash, a camera shove, a flinch and blood.

    Gameplay
  28. Hour 19

    Three sets rebuilt in an evening

    After the first playtest the Director had the zombie, UFO and noir levels rebuilt as three connected spaces each, with props made by script in Blender and a lighting pass, and the 23:30 reels show all three.

    World and visuals
  29. Hour 21

    Scene three, from the slate to the dailies

    The 01:30 reel runs Dead Mile Motel end to end for the first time: the script page ticks its lines off, the stage door opens, and the replay stars the stunt double instead of a mannequin.

    Gameplay
  30. Hour 22

    The night shift: how the crew works while I sleep

    I signed off at 22:30 with a playtest booked for 07:00; every two hours since, the engine has filmed every level, a reviewer has marked what's broken, and the Director has sent the fixes out before the next block.

    Behind the scenes
  31. Hour 23

    Too many editors, one machine

    At 02:50 the machine ran out of memory with several headless Unreal runs going at once; it took down the editor and two sessions, and the crew now runs one heavy job at a time.

    Bloopers
  32. Hour 24

    At 4 a.m. a bot played the level for the camera

    The reels used to fake their kills by console command; at 03:46 a new autoplayer from the gameplay session played Dead Mile Motel with real inputs, died once, finished the script and walked out through the stage door, after staring at the tanker for 86 seconds.

    Gameplay
  33. Hour 24

    The menus, from the design boards to the build

    On Thursday morning Claude Design drew the pause screen and the Dailies card as boards; by the 03:30 reel the UI session had built both, and a polish pass held every screen up against its board.

    Interface

Day 2, Fri 13 Nov

17 posts
  1. Hour 27

    Playtest two: the enemies turned up, and they aren't smart enough

    I played the build the crew froze at 06:00 after a night on its own. Eight notes came out of it, starting with dumb enemies, a UFO level with no way in, and a frame rate that has to reach 60.

    Gameplay
  2. Hour 28

    Lumen is out: finding seven milliseconds a frame

    My playtest ran at about 20 fps. Within the hour the feel session had measured what every graphics setting costs on Zombie 01, and the Director switched Lumen off for good.

    Bloopers
  3. Hour 30

    Tape on the floor: my playtest notes, three hours on

    Four of this morning's eight playtest notes were on film by 10:00, starting with a trail of tape marks to the objective. Six of the seven levels now finish by their own script.

    Gameplay
  4. Hour 31

    The lock nobody was holding

    An empty git lock file has blocked every commit in the shared checkout at least ten times since last night. The tool that's allowed to delete it failed its first review, then starved, then learned to ask who is older.

    Bloopers
  5. Hour 33

    The diner has a door

    UFO 01 was impossible at 07:28 because nothing could get into the diner. On the 12:30 reel the autoplayer walks in, finds the fuse, and leaves through the stage door to a CUT! PRINT IT card.

    World and visuals
  6. Hour 33

    What the crew would cost at API prices

    About $1,200 so far for 33 hours of a fourteen-session crew, if it were billed per token. $577 of that is measured; the rest is an estimate with its working shown. The figure on this site now updates every time it publishes.

    Behind the scenes
  7. Hour 35

    An art pass without downloading a texture

    At 11:26 I put "art passes, possibly new art" on the plan. By 15:30 every set had new surfaces, decals, posters and props, all made by script, with no downloaded textures. The stunt double finally has a helmet that reads on film.

    World and visuals
  8. Hour 36

    The double stays on its mark

    My playtest note said the replay double ran off in directions I never went, then snapped back. The cause was in the animations, not the recording, and the old test was checking the wrong thing.

    Gameplay
  9. Hour 36

    Milestone: the first package you can actually play from the zip

    The packaging milestone was "met a day early" on Thursday evening. It took seven more packages and most of Friday before one could start a run from the title screen and play three scenes through.

    Milestones
  10. Hour 38

    Playtest three: better, faster, and far too easy

    Twenty minutes on the 18:00 package: the frame rate and the replays held up, but the fights are too easy, so tonight's work is a difficulty pass, no guns for zombies, and a clearer tutorial.

    Gameplay
  11. Hour 38

    The details: ten shots of the sets, up close

    In the 17:30 filming block the engine pointed the camera at props instead of gameplay: the stage door lighting up, pumps, posters, a lead zombie. Half the shots worked; the rest show how blind the script is.

    World and visuals
  12. Hour 40

    Too easy: what the crew changed in the next ninety minutes

    At 18:38 I said the game was too easy. By 20:05 the weapons were weaker, the zombies tougher, the guns gone from the zombie levels, a spitter zombie built, and the tutorial target turned into a cardboard stunt double.

    Gameplay
  13. Hour 41

    Art pass three: the second levels catch up

    The motel, the farm, the rooftops and the finale's soundstage were still part grey box at 17:00. By 21:00 the second World session had replaced the last blocks with a windmill, a scarecrow, a motel office with a neon sign, pigeon lofts and flight cases, all built by script.

    World and visuals
  14. Hour 42

    Milestone: from the title screen to THAT'S A WRAP

    The 23:00 milestone asked for a player to get from the start to an ending. The ending chain played unskipped on film at 21:30, and at 22:43 the packaged build ran from the title to the wrap and back in one go.

    Milestones
  15. Hour 44

    On film: the cardboard double falls, and the spitter spits

    Two of tonight's answers to playtest three finally made it onto film at 23:30, on the third try for one of them: the stunt double cutout in the screen test, and the spitter zombie's lob.

    Gameplay
  16. Hour 47

    The first frame of every scene, reframed

    Overnight the second World session treated each level's starting view as a film's opening shot, lit and framed around the hero piece, and the World session set the rain, steam and bulbs running on game time.

    World and visuals
  17. Hour 47

    Every shot sees the double: the replay cameras get rules

    The replay cameras used to film walls, empty rooms and the inside of the set. Overnight the second Gameplay session gave them rules, tested them, and smoothed the double's animation, and the 01:30 Noir 01 replay shows it.

    Gameplay

Day 3, Sat 14 Nov

11 posts
  1. Hour 49

    Night shift two: what the crew did with "too easy

    I handed over at 18:38 with five notes and asked for fourteen hours of work. By 05:00 the crew had made 188 commits, answered all five, hit the 23:00 milestone early, and left me a build to play and a list of decisions to check.

    Behind the scenes
  2. Hour 53

    Take two broke three ways in one night

    The retake was too easy, then impossible, then haunted by a frozen killer. Three bugs in the same eight hours, each one hidden behind the last, and the 05:30 reel shows a retake that finally starts the scene again properly.

    Bloopers
  3. Hour 54

    Playtest four: I played the whole film, start to credits

    Saturday morning I played the packaged build from the title to the credits for the first time, 8 scenes in 16 takes and about ten minutes of film. My verdict was "really is coming together", followed by 16 notes.

    Gameplay
  4. Hour 56

    The same door on every set

    Every level ends at a stage door with a ring of marquee bulbs, and the rule is that it reads from eight metres. I posted the gallery, then the independent review failed three of the doors.

    World and visuals
  5. Hour 59

    The stop sign was edge-on: nine held weapons, re-posed by arithmetic

    I said the held weapons looked misaligned. The third Gameplay session photographed all nine against a blank wall, worked out each new pose from where it should sit on screen, and the engine photographed them again a few hours later.

    Gameplay
  6. Hour 60

    Standing still stops being safe

    In a game where time slows when you stop, stopping was the winning move. The Enemies session added idle pressure, the first film of it failed on the real levels, and the 14:45 reel shows a player who stands still dying in every level filmed.

    Gameplay
  7. Hour 60

    What is this scene about? One line on the slate

    I finished the whole game this morning without knowing what any scene was about. Six hours later each scene opens with one handwritten line of story on the slate and calls out the objective in a band, on film in two of the three levels checked.

    Interface
  8. Hour 61

    Every tab gone, back in ten minutes

    At 17:27 the editor ran out of memory during a menus kit and Windows closed every crew session with it; all eleven were reopened fresh by 17:38 and picked up from their desk notes.

    Bloopers
  9. Hour 63

    Playtest five: just show the whole replay

    An hour after the crash I played the lunchtime build and typed one long note; four minutes later it was eight tasks, and the replay fix was coded ten minutes after that.

    Gameplay
  10. Hour 66

    Milestone: it's all in the build, and the review still failed

    Saturday's 23:00 milestone asked for real UI, audio, enemy behaviour, level progression and lighting in the build; the 21:00 package has all five and plays from the title to the credits, and the Director's reviewers failed both halves of the evening's film anyway.

    Milestones
  11. Hour 70

    The gas pumps finally blow up on camera

    The barrels and gas pumps had been rigged to explode since Friday, but no film caught one until the 01:00 reels, after a tag mix-up, a camera that cut too early and a pass to stop them reading as glowing domes.

    Bloopers

Day 4, Sun 15 Nov

1 post
  1. Hour 73

    Night shift three: the enemies stop walking away

    I went to bed at 23:08 with "pathfinding still broken". By 05:35 the crew had found the root cause, made 75 commits across six merge windows and passed three packages, and the replay was still the one thing that wouldn't behave.

    Behind the scenes

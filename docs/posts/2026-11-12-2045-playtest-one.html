I played it for the first time, and no enemies showed up

While the crew was stopped by the third usage stall I played the game for an hour. Every reel today had zombies in it. My playthrough had none, and that wasn't the only thing I'd change.

Day 1, Thu 12 NovHour 1620:45

  • Me
  • Director

The third usage stall hit at 19:07. The crew stopped. The editor didn't, so I sat down and played Reel Time for the first time, from about 19:10 to 20:20, with nobody to report bugs to.

The blocker

Fourteen hours of reels, tests and reviews all showed enemies. Then a person played it. From my notes to the Director when it came back:

"In each of the levels, no enemy spawned at any point. It was just a case of basically walking to the X each time."

And sometimes not even that:

"sometimes I wouldn't be near the X, or I wouldn't necessarily know where the X is, and it would end anyway."

An hour before I sat down, the Engine had checked every level and logged enemies spawning in all six. I don't know yet why my run was different. That went to the Gameplay session as the top task the minute the crew was back.

It's the same lesson as the HUD this morning and the replay this afternoon, one step further along. A test passed, a reel looked right, and the thing a player does first didn't work.

What I liked

  • "I thought the UI was good."
  • The replay camera "is decent", though I want it a bit further from the player.
  • "The theming and styles and art is all fine. It's all actually fairly decent. It doesn't obviously need a big art pass."

That last one changes the plan more than it sounds. The next push is pacing, not polish.

What I asked for

A script, not a quest log. The game doesn't tell you what to do. My note: "maybe we can style it like a movie script, so they're actually kind of reading the script." Objectives become lines of a screenplay.

Light, not a compass. "I'm not sure this game suits a top compass, honestly… maybe by using light and light colors to denote direction."

A door, not an X. The exit is a red X on the floor, like an actor's mark. It makes sense and I couldn't find it. "I want it to be like a door frame with a bright light in it."

Bigger levels. "We need starting, and a middle and end. 60 seconds to a minute and a half." Several spaces per level, not one room.

A player character. The replay shows Unreal's default mannequin. It needs to be somebody, with third-person animations for the replay.

Music that follows the film. The music is slowed by an effect when time slows. I want a track that simply plays at the speed of the film.

What the Director did with it

I ended my notes with "You need to let me know what you think." Within half an hour the Director had written a new contract for the objectives system and handed out the work:

  • the no-enemies blocker, then the script objectives, to Gameplay
  • a stage door, a follow spot and route lights to World
  • the gas station rebuilt as three spaces to the second World session
  • a script-page HUD to UI, the tutorial rewritten as a script to the second Gameplay session
  • a third-person animation set to the NPC session, after the player character

It also made two calls without asking me and logged them as assumptions: the player's costume colours, and three levels first with the finale as a stretch.

One note it pushed back on. I'd suggested a well-known film's outfit as a reference for the player. The jam's rules don't allow other people's IP, so the Director's answer was a stunt double's tracksuit in the game's own colours.

Update, 21:05: why there were no enemies

The Gameplay session reproduced it by doing what I did: start at the title and travel from level to level.

The levels are built on World Partition, Unreal's system for streaming a map in piece by piece. After travelling from one level to the next, the enemy spawn points and the exit mark streamed in a moment after the scene had started. So every wave spawned nothing, the scene counted itself as cleared, and it ended. That's also why it sometimes ended when I was nowhere near the X.

Open a level directly in the editor and everything is loaded before play starts. Every reel today, and the Engine's level-by-level check, opened levels that way. I was the first to arrive at a level the way a player does.

The fixes: the flow now waits for its spawn points before it starts, and the level kits mark gameplay actors as always loaded. The sets are only 30 to 60 metres across, so there was never much to stream. And the full-game test now travels like a player and checks that enemies are alive in every level.

The Director's one-line version:

"we tested the levels, not the journey between them."

The crew hit the usage limit and went dark for two hours

Three hours into the jam the whole crew ran out of usage. A 19-minute warning meant nothing was lost, but it cost two hours, a filming block, and a restart that failed the first time.

Day 1, Thu 12 NovHour 610:30

  • Director
  • Engine
  • Me

Eleven Claude Code sessions working at once use a lot. A little after 08:00, three hours into the jam, I could see the usage limit coming, and told the Director.

The warning

The Director mailed every session at 08:06, marked urgent:

"Right now, before your next step: (1) commit everything you have in your own paths (work in progress is fine: say WIP in the message) … (2) add a note to your current task saying exactly where you are and the next step … (3) then carry on working as normal until the limit stops you. Do NOT park or pause for this."

The Engine got its own line: leave the editor running, and if a merge is half done, "finish or revert to a green build first".

That last part mattered. At 08:02 the main build had broken: two gameplay sessions had each defined the same struct on their own branches. The fix was merged at 08:11, with the limit minutes away. The crew stopped on a build that compiles.

The stop

The last commit landed at 08:25. Then nothing for 1 hour 56 minutes.

What it cost:

  • Two hours of a 93-hour jam, for every session at once.
  • The 09:30 filming block. 22 finished tasks were already queued for the Engine to film. They're still waiting, so there's no new footage in this post.

What it didn't cost: any work. Every session had committed and left a note for whoever picked the task up next.

Back on, the second time

At 10:20 the limit reset and I told the Director to fire the team back up. One session was still alive and committed within a minute.

The other nine didn't come back. The resume script was meant to reopen each session in its own conversation. The Director's log, seven minutes later:

"resume_all closed the sessions but the resumed tabs never started; started all nine fresh with spawn.py (they pick up from task notes)."

So the conversations were gone, and the notes were all that was left. That turned out to be enough: by 10:28 all nine were running again, each starting from its task file.

The next filming block is 11:30. Everything outstanding gets filmed then.

What I'd change

The warning worked because I happened to be watching. The crew can't see the limit coming on its own, so the habit now is the one the warning forced: commit small, and keep the task note current, so a stop at any moment loses nothing.

Update, 15:25: it happened again

The crew ran from 10:21 to 14:13 and made 163 commits. Then the limit arrived a second time. The last commit was at 14:13, and the reset came at about 15:20: a second stall of a little over an hour.

This time the warning went out at 14:07 with one change. The first restart had shown that this machine keeps no record of each session's conversation, so nothing can be resumed. The Director's message said so plainly:

"after the reset you are restarted as a FRESH session that has only your task logs, desk note and inbox"

So every session also wrote a desk note: what it was doing, the gotchas, where things live. The filming block moved from 15:30 to 16:00 to make room.

The notes weren't needed. At 15:21 every session came back in place, mid-conversation, and carried on. They're still the right habit: the first restart didn't go that way.

Two stalls, about three hours in total, out of the first ten and a half.

Update, 20:25: and a third time

The last commit before the third stop was at 19:05. The crew came back at about 20:20, another hour and a quarter gone. It landed right on the slot I'd set aside to play the game for the first time.

That makes three stalls and a little over four hours dark in the first sixteen. Each one cost less than the one before: the first needed a failed restart and nine fresh sessions, the second and third just picked up mid-sentence.
